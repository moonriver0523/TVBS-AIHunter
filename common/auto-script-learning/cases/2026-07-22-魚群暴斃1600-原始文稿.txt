A HEAT WAVE IN MINNESOTA IS IMPACTING WATER QUALITY IN THE LAKES -- AND KILLING OFF FISH.

AS DEREK JAMES REPORTS -- HUNDREDS OF FISH HAVE DIED DUE TO THE HIGH WATER TEMPERATURE.

--REPORTER PKG-AS FOLLOWS--

nats

THE EFFECTS OF THIS PROLONGED HEAT WAVE ARE BECOMING IMPOSSIBLE TO MISS.

ALONG THE SHORELINE OF PELTIER LAKE IN CENTERVILLE, HUNDREDS OF DEAD FISH - MOSTLY NORTHERN PIKE ALONG WITH A FEW BLUEGILL AND LARGEMOUTH BASS - ARE WASHING UP AFTER 10 DAYS OF 90+ DEGREES TEMPERATURES.

nats

IN NORTHERN WISCONSIN'S LAKE NAMAKAGON, DEAD MUSKIES ARE SURFACING.

nats

DNR CREWS ARE MEASURING LAKE CONDITIONS AFTER NUMEROUS FISH KILLS...

nats

THAT WORKS OUT TO ABOUT 88 DEGREES - A WATER TEMPERATURE CONSIDERED A CHRONIC STRESS LEVEL FOR FISH.

Dr. Isaiah Tolo, Minnesota Department of Natural Resources "Today we're at 202 reports covering 99 water bodies across the state. And a huge pulse of these has really been just in the last week."

WARM WATER HOLDS LESS OXYGEN, MAKING IT HARDER FOR FISH TO SURVIVE.

Dr. Isaiah Tolo, Minnesota Department of Natural Resources: "I think were looking at a bunch of shiners. I cant tell if they're here eating or if they're here because they're stressed."

LARGER FISH LIKE NORTHERN PIKE AND MUSKIES ARE ESPECIALLY VULNERABLE.

Dr. Tolo: "This water is Pea soup green, there's algae and other bacteria in this water that are using up oxygen. And as the water gets warmer, the amount of oxygen that can actually be present in that water is going to be lower."

nats

FISHING GUIDE CHRIS GRIFFIN SAYS THAT'S WHY HE'S PUTTING MUSKY TRIPS ON HOLD.

Chris Griffin, Fishing Guide: "This year is by far the warmest I've seen on Minnetonka. I had 91.1 degrees last Thursday, averages in that 85-ish range."

HE'S WAITING FOR COOLER WATER TO BEFORE LETTING CLIENTS CHASE THEM AGAIN.

Chris Griffin, Fishing Guide: "I won't book them for muskies until the fall."

AND HE'S HOPING OTHER ANGLERS WILL SHOW THE SAME RESTRAINT UNTIL CONDITIONS IMPROVE.

--TAG--

OFFICIALS SAY PEOPLE SHOULD ALSO AVOID WADING IN AREAS WHERE LARGE NUMBERS OF FISH HAVE DIED BECAUSE THE DECOMPOSING FISH CAN POSE HEALTH RISKS.
